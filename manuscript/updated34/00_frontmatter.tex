\begin{abstract}
\noindent Universities routinely revise intellectual-property policies governing invention disclosure and commercialization, yet little is known about what follows. We study documented revisions and compare technology-transfer outcomes around changes toward more supportive or restrictive communication. Licensing outcomes diverge after revision, with more supportive revisions associated with stronger licensing relative to more restrictive revisions and contemporaneous non-revisers. Evidence for changes in patenting is much weaker, and we do not detect a corresponding increase in invention disclosure. The results point to the licensing stage while remaining observational because universities choose how they revise their policies.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} university technology transfer; intellectual-property policy; licensing; policy communication; policy revisions.\\
