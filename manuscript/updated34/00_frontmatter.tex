\begin{abstract}
\noindent Universities regularly rewrite the intellectual-property policies that govern invention disclosure, ownership,
and commercialization, yet little is known about what follows these ordinary revisions. We identify 127 changes in
observed policy histories at AUTM-linked U.S. research universities using the Policy Communication Stance Index (PCSI),
apply outcome-support and non-overlap screens, and manually review every one of the 62 revisions mechanically capable of
entering the event study. Thirty-four revisions satisfy common document-comparability and timing criteria, eight toward
more supportive communication and 26 toward more restrictive communication. In a stacked event study, the average
post-revision licensing difference between upward and downward revisions is 0.495 log points (clustered SE 0.140;
100,000-draw direction-label permutation $p=0.032$), with no statistically detectable pre-revision trend difference
($p=0.32$). A stricter 29-event sample requiring an observed calendar year after revision yields 0.499 (SE 0.133;
$p=0.026$). Filing and patent-application measures move in the same direction but imprecisely, while invention
disclosures do not increase. Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment across five outcomes gives the preferred licensing result
$q=0.159$. Because revision direction is chosen by universities and upward events remain few, the evidence documents a
robust post-revision pattern concentrated at the licensing margin rather than a causal effect of policy language.
\end{abstract}
