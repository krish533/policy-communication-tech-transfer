\begin{abstract}
\noindent Universities routinely revise intellectual-property policies governing invention disclosure and commercialization, yet little is known about what follows these changes. We study documented policy revisions and compare technology-transfer outcomes around revisions that shift communication toward more supportive or more restrictive positions. Licensing outcomes diverge after revision, with more supportive revisions associated with stronger licensing performance relative to more restrictive revisions and contemporaneous non-revisers. We find much weaker evidence for changes in patenting and no corresponding increase in invention disclosure. The results point to the licensing stage of technology transfer while remaining observational because universities choose how they revise their policies.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} university technology transfer; intellectual-property policy; licensing; policy communication; policy revisions.\\
